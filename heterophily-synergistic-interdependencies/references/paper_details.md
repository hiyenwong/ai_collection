## Abstract
This paper reveals heterophily as a minimal local adaptive mechanism that generates self-organized synergistic interdependencies in adaptive systems. Through analytical solutions for minimal three-node systems, it demonstrates how heterophily weakens pairwise dependencies while inducing higher-order dependencies via geometric constraints, creating synergistic information dynamics in systems like brains, social networks, and ecosystems.

## Key Contributions
1. Analytical solution for N=3 system showing heterophily-synergy mechanism
2. Information decomposition framework for quantifying synergistic effects
3. Demonstration across multiple domains: neural, social, ecological
