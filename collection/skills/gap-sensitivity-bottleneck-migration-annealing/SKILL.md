# Gap Sensitivity and Bottleneck Migration in Sparse Constraint-Preserving Quantum Annealing

**Paper**: Gap Sensitivity and Bottleneck Migration in Sparse Constraint-Preserving Quantum Annealing (arXiv:2610.11262, Oct 2026)
**Authors**: Aruna Gupta, S. R. Hassan (IMSc Chennai, HBNI)
**Activation**: quantum annealing driver design, minimum spectral gap, constraint-preserving mixer, XY mixer, bottleneck migration, relinearization, Dirichlet form, exchange Laplacian, fixed-cardinality QUBO

## Core Methodology

Central question: for a fixed-cardinality constrained problem with a **constraint-preserving exchange (XY-type) driver**, many coupling graphs preserve the same feasible sector and share the same Dicke ground state — which exchange modification most raises the **minimum spectral gap** along the annealing path?

### 1. Exchange-Resolved Gap Sensitivity (Proposition 1)

For annealing path H(s,w) = (1−s)D(w) + sP with normalized driver D(w) = A(w)/Λ(w), Λ = λ_max[A]:

```
∂J/∂w_e = (1−s*)/Λ · [G_e(s*) − ℓ_e δD(s*)]
```

where at the active minimum s*:
- **G_e(s) = ⟨ψ₁|L_e|ψ₁⟩ − ⟨ψ₀|L_e|ψ₀⟩** — Dirichlet-energy contrast between first-excited and ground states on the swaps generated by physical edge e. Since L_e = I − SWAP_ij is a graph Laplacian, ⟨ψ|L_e|ψ⟩ = Σ|ψ(x)−ψ(y)|² over configuration swaps across e. G_e measures how strongly exchange channel e distinguishes the two states forming the current bottleneck.
- **ℓ_e = ⟨φ_Λ|L_e|φ_Λ⟩**, δD = ⟨ψ₁|D|ψ₁⟩−⟨ψ₀|D|ψ₀⟩ — the driver-normalization correction (Hellmann–Feynman: ∂_e Λ = ℓ_e)
- Envelope theorem evaluates at s* without differentiating s* itself
- Edge replacement (remove r, add a) must be scored as ONE direction δA = L_a − L_r — at degenerate bandwidths the derivative is not linear, so add/remove scores cannot be combined independently

**Endpoint ceiling & abstention**: ∆(1) is driver-independent, so when s*=1 is the unique active minimum the derivative vanishes — the oracle correctly abstains (102/900 dense, 90/900 frustrated starts).

**Degenerate first-excited level**: E₁' via λ_min[U₁†VU₁] with V=(1−s)Ḋ — diagonalize perturbation within the degenerate eigenspace.

### 2. Bottleneck Migration — "repair-and-expose" mechanism

The finite-step failure mode: a locally favorable direction raises the current bottleneck until a **different coexisting local minimum of ∆(s)** becomes competitive and takes over as the global limiter.

Dense quadratic family (mean–variance-inspired, N=6, K=3, 30 objectives, 900 starts):
- All 798 non-abstained directions improve at α=10⁻³, 10⁻² (infinitesimal regime: 100% reliable)
- Improvement decays: 99.75% @ α=0.05 → 96.87% @ 0.50 → **82.96% at complete edge replacement** (α=1)
- Resolved bottleneck exchange on 52.8% of paths; on 420/421 switching paths the first switch occurs AFTER the gap already rose above its initial value — the direction first does exactly what the sensitivity predicted, then exposes a competitor
- Subspace overlap O_sub = Tr(Π_before Π_after)/2: median **0.449 on switching paths vs 0.915 without** — migration is accompanied by strong low-energy subspace reorganization
- At 136/279 interior exchange events the departing branch RISES while the incoming branch FALLS along the original direction — the direction keeps doing its old job on a bottleneck that is no longer the one controlling J

Sparse frustrated Ising family (mixed-sign, triangular-prism, same protocol): same sequence (all 809 improve infinitesimally; switching 23.2%, lower frequency; overlap 0.478 vs 0.962) — **mechanism is family-independent even though switching frequency is not**.

Migration is neither necessary nor sufficient for a harmful full replacement (harmful: 24.0% of switching, 9.3% of non-switching paths) — it is the identifiable mechanism, not the whole story.

### 3. Relinearization: active-minima max–min rule (Eq. 24)

Once two minima are co-active at driver w_c with active set A_c:
```
d_act ∈ arg max_{d∈D_c∪{0}} min_{b∈A_c} g_b(d)
```
D_c = ordered unit weight-transfers between supported edges; zero direction allows abstention. This protects ALL co-active minima to first order (lower-envelope response).

At 279 interior exchange events (27 objectives), step ε=10⁻²:
| Rule | Improve | Harm | Mean r |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continue original | 108 | 171 | −0.0021 |
| Incoming minimum only | 164 | 115 | +0.0002 |
| **Both active minima (max–min)** | **278** | **0** | **+0.0033** |

Robust at ε=10⁻³ (278 improve, 0 harm). The lesson: at a bottleneck crossing the minimum gap is locally a **lower-envelope problem** — you cannot patch continuation by optimizing only the newly active branch.

### 4. Classical approximations (diagnostics, not replacements)

- **Spectral (initial-gap)**: Caputo–Liggett–Richthammer interchange-process identity gap A(w) = λ₂[L_S(w)] gives exact ∆(0) and an upper bound J ≤ C(S,w) — initial connectivity only, not the interior bottleneck. Rank-one edge additions CANNOT raise a degenerate Fiedler eigenvalue to first order (kills spectral selector on cycle/star geometries).
- **Two-state crossing ansatz**: |ψ₀⟩≈cosθ|a⟩+sinθ|b⟩ gives G_e ≈ 2sin2θ for exchanges directly connecting competing configurations a,b — effective near localized avoided crossings.
- **Basin coupling score** F_e = 2Σ u_A(x)u_B(y) with γ=8 basin amplitudes (Eq. 37-38): practical failure mode is **basin-pair identification** — correct pair → median Q=1.000, incorrect pair → Q=0.080; substituting the oracle pair on the same instances lifts median to 1.000 (paired gain 0.735). Neither spectral nor problem-informed selectors dominate uniformly across N∈{7,8,10}×{cycle, star+edge, path+chord}.

### 5. Discrete search evidence (deliberately weaker)

Exhaustive N=6 reference (21,766 graphs, 652,980 graph–instance evaluations): guided search (rank all legal replacements by sensitivity, evaluate top-8 finite gaps) hits the numerical optimum at median R=1 for all budgets B=5..9; guided-vs-evaluation-cap-matched-random advantage only significant at B≥7 (δR ≈ +0.006..+0.010, bootstrap CI above zero). All-neighbor search itself misses the exhaustive optimum on some instances — local information alone never guarantees a globally optimal trajectory.

## Reusable Patterns

1. **Dirichlet-energy contrast as a local design signal**: for any constraint-preserving Laplacian driver, the first-order gap response of a structural edit factorizes per physical channel as an energy contrast between the two bottleneck states — a physically interpretable, cheap gradient surrogate.

2. **Repair-and-expose / bottleneck migration**: optimizing away the current bottleneck systematically exposes the next one. Expect this whenever the objective is a min over multiple coexisting quasi-independent failure modes (gap minima, constraint violations, robustness margins). Local derivative information is reliable exactly as far as the active set is unchanged.

3. **Active-set max–min relinearization**: when failure modes tie, re-derive the local direction as argmax_d min_{active} response(d) — protecting the whole lower envelope. Beats both "keep going" and "switch to the new mode" heuristics (278/279 vs 108/279 and 164/279).

4. **Subspace-overlap diagnostic**: rank-two projector overlap Tr(Π_b Π_a)/2 quantifies how much a finite design change reorganizes the relevant low-energy sector — a model-agnostic warning signal for when local linearization is about to break.

5. **Abstention-by-envelope**: if the active minimum sits at a driver-independent endpoint, the correct first-order response is to abstain — encode abstention (zero direction) in the update menu rather than forcing a move.

## Scope Caveats (authors' own)

Small feasible sectors, exact normalization, exhaustive landscapes, ORACLE low-energy states as benchmark resources — establishes a **finite-size mechanism**, not a scalable prescription, not finite-time speedup, not quantum advantage. Scalable extensions need: detection of competing bottlenecks from accessible information, active-set response estimation without full diagonalization, and step-size control preserving local validity.
