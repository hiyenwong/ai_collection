# EWL Quantum Game Circuits for Economic Innovation

## Description

A parameterized 4-qubit Eisert-Wilkens-Lewenstein (EWL) quantum game circuit for recommender systems in quadruple helix innovation ecosystems (academia, industry, government, civil society). Maps quantum game measurement probabilities to Dirac-Solow-Swan Hamiltonian for simulating capital accumulation and bifurcation dynamics under disruptive innovation.

## Core Innovation

Bridges quantum game theory, parameterized quantum circuits, and relativistic economic growth models — offering a computationally efficient tool for innovation policy and strategic decision-making in complex socio-economic ecosystems.

## Methodology

### Step 1: EWL Quantum Game Circuit Construction

- 4-qubit EWL circuit (one qubit per helix actor)
- Local strategy operators: U_i = R_y(θ_i)
- Multi-qubit EWL entangler → parameterized local rotations → inverse entangler → full measurement
- Circuit depth: 11, total gates: 22, scales as O(n) for n-round communications

### Step 2: Parameter Calibration from Real Data

- Extract dominance weights from participant funding data (ecContribution)
- European Commission CORDIS Horizon Europe database as source
- Normalize weights to control rotation angles θ_i
- Each actor's strategic position encoded in quantum state

### Step 3: Measurement and Scoring

- Measurement probabilities serve as recommender scores
- Scores distinguish disruptive vs sustaining innovation trends
- Probabilities directly computable on NISQ hardware

### Step 4: Dirac-Solow-Swan Hamiltonian Mapping

- Map measurement probabilities to diagonal Dirac potential
- Dirac-Solow-Swan Hamiltonian: relativistic economic growth model
- Time-evolution simulation of capital accumulation
- Captures bifurcation dynamics under disruptive innovation

## When to Use

- Innovation policy analysis in multi-stakeholder ecosystems
- Recommender systems for disruptive vs sustaining innovation
- Quantum game theory applied to economic decision-making
- Strategic planning in quadruple helix networks
- Capital accumulation modeling under uncertainty

## Pitfalls

### NISQ Compatibility

The circuit uses only 4 qubits with depth 11 — designed for NISQ-era execution. However, real-world calibration requires access to EC CORDIS funding data.

### Simplified Economic Model

The Dirac-Solow-Swan Hamiltonian is a stylized growth model. Results should be interpreted as qualitative insights, not quantitative forecasts.

### Data Requirements

Calibration requires structured funding data with participation weights. Without real data, the model defaults to uniform rotations.
