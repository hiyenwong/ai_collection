# Quantum Statistical Modeling via Self-Adjoint Statistical Models

## Core Framework

This methodology extends quantum sufficiency beyond conventional complex *-algebraic
settings to real *-subalgebras and real Jordan algebras, enabling analysis of general
self-adjoint operators including derivatives of states.

## Key Concepts

### 1. Self-Adjoint Likelihood-Type Objects

Square-root likelihood ratios and symmetric logarithmic derivatives serve as fundamental
self-adjoint objects in quantum statistical models:

- **Square-root likelihood ratio**: `L_ρ(σ) = ρ^{-1/2} σ ρ^{-1/2}` for states ρ, σ
- **Symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD)**: `dρ_θ/dθ = (L_θ ρ_θ + ρ_θ L_θ) / 2`

These objects naturally arise when extending sufficiency to real structures.

### 2. Sufficient Real Positive Maps

Three levels of sufficiency correspond to three map types:

| Structure | Map Type | Properties |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Complex *-subalgebra | Complex CP maps | Standard quantum sufficiency |
| Real *-subalgebra | Real CP maps | Self-adjoint closure |
| Real Jordan algebra | Real positive maps | Minimal sufficient structure |

### 3. Minimal Sufficient Characterization

Minimal sufficient real *-subalgebras are characterized by:
- The **likelihood-ratio set** `{L_ρ(σ)}`
- **ρ-modular invariance**: invariance under the modular automorphism group σ^ρ_t
- The real Jordan algebra generated by these objects is the minimal sufficient structure

### 4. Koashi-Imoto Decomposition

For sufficient real *-subalgebras and real Jordan algebras:
- Decomposes the Hilbert space into a direct sum of tensor products
- Separates the classical (commutative) and quantum (non-commutative) parts
- Generalizes the standard KI decomposition to real structures

## Implementation Workflow

1. **Define the statistical model**: Specify the family of self-adjoint operators {A_θ}
2. **Construct likelihood-type objects**: Compute square-root ratios and SLDs
3. **Identify sufficient structure**: Find the minimal real *-subalgebra containing all likelihood ratios
4. **Apply modular invariance**: Verify ρ-modular invariance of the candidate algebra
5. **Perform KI decomposition**: Decompose into classical/quantum components
6. **Validate sufficiency**: Check that the reduced model preserves all statistical information

## Mathematical Background

### Real Jordan Algebras

A real Jordan algebra (J, ∘) satisfies:
- Commutativity: `a ∘ b = b ∘ a`
- Jordan identity: `(a ∘ b) ∘ (a ∘ a) = a ∘ (b ∘ (a ∘ a))`

For self-adjoint operators: `A ∘ B = (AB + BA) / 2`

### Quantum Fisher Information

The quantum Fisher information metric relates to the SLD:
`I_Q(θ) = Tr[ρ_θ L_θ²]`

This metric is naturally defined on the real Jordan algebra of sufficient statistics.

## Applications

- **Quantum parameter estimation**: Optimal measurement design via sufficient statistics
- **Quantum channel discrimination**: Minimal sufficient structures for distinguishing channels
- **Quantum state tomography**: Reduced tomography using sufficient subalgebras
- **Quantum machine learning**: Feature selection via quantum sufficiency

## Pitfalls

- Degenerate reference states require careful treatment of support projections
- The likelihood-ratio set may be uncountable — use generating sets
- Real Jordan algebras are NOT associative; avoid assuming (A∘B)∘C = A∘(B∘C)
- Complex CP maps are strictly more restrictive than real positive maps
