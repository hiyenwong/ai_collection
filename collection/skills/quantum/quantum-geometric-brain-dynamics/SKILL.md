# Quantum Geometric Brain Dynamics

## Overview

Quantum geometric methods apply information geometry, Fisher information, and quantum-inspired probability frameworks to model brain network dynamics. This approach treats neural state spaces as manifolds with intrinsic geometric structure, enabling more principled analysis of brain activity patterns.

## Core Concepts

### Information Geometry for Neural States
- **Statistical manifold**: Brain states as points on a manifold
- **Fisher information metric**: Defines distance between neural states
- **Geodesic paths**: Optimal transitions between brain states

### Quantum-Inspired Probability
- **Density matrices**: Represent mixed neural states
- **Quantum entropy**: Measure of neural uncertainty
- **Quantum mutual information**: Captures non-classical correlations

### Geometric Deep Learning
- **Manifold neural networks**: Respect intrinsic geometry
- **Geodesic convolutions**: Local operations on neural manifolds
- **Curvature-aware learning**: Adapt to manifold structure

- Paper: arXiv:2604.15567 (2026-04-17)

## Workflow

### Step 1: State Space Construction
- Embed neural data in statistical manifold
- Compute Fisher information metric
- Estimate manifold curvature

### Step 2: Geometric Analysis
- Compute geodesic distances between states
- Analyze curvature along neural trajectories
- Identify critical points (phase transitions)

### Step 3: Quantum-Inspired Modeling
- Construct density matrix representations
- Compute quantum entropy and mutual information
- Detect non-classical correlations

### Step 4: Geometric Deep Learning
- Build manifold-aware neural networks
- Train with geodesic convolutions
- Validate on classification/regression tasks

## Applications

- Brain state classification (sleep stages, cognitive states)
- Neural trajectory prediction
- Brain-computer interfaces
- Neurological disorder detection

## Pitfalls

- **Metric estimation**: Fisher information requires sufficient data
- **Computational cost**: Geodesic computation is expensive
- **Manifold assumption**: May not hold for all neural data
- **Interpretation**: Geometric concepts require domain expertise

## Verification Steps

1. Validate metric estimation on synthetic data
2. Check geodesic consistency across trajectories
3. Verify quantum mutual information bounds
4. Compare with Euclidean baselines
