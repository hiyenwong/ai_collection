# Beyond Selected Breakthroughs: Evaluating Language Models on Randomly Sampled Mathematical Conjectures

**Authors:** Helen Jenne, Davis Brown, Jesse He, Henry Kvinge
**Published:** 2026-10-29
**arXiv:** [2610.40382v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40382v1)
**Categories:** cs.AI, math.HO
**Utility Score:** 0.88

## Abstract

The last year has seen a shift in AI-assisted mathematics, with frontier language models producing proofs of and counterexamples to long-standing open conjectures. However, studying only the current set of major breakthroughs provides an incomplete picture of LLM capabilities and the computational resources needed to achieve reported results. To address this, we introduce a new dataset, OpenConjecture, which collects conjectures from mathematics papers published on the arXiv since February 2026. We use these conjectures as a source of open mathematical problems, and apply frontier GPT models to a random sample of 200 of these conjectures over three passes using a reproducible protocol without problem-specific mathematical guidance. Rather than attempt expert verification of these candidate solutions, we analyze the types of problems for which LLMs produce solutions and the characteristics of these solutions. The models we test produce candidate solutions for roughly 60% of the conjectures, with counterexamples about 1.3 times more common than proofs. We estimate solution novelty, assess how solutions cite and relate to existing literature, and analyze whether the proposed resolution relies on either an error in or underspecification of the conjecture.
