# Quantum Game Theory for Economics

## Core Concepts

### 1. Volunteer's Dilemma (Classical)

The Volunteer's Dilemma describes a group of n players who decide whether to:
- **Volunteer**: Incur a personal cost C to provide a collective benefit B
- **Abstain**: Risk forfeiting the group reward if no one volunteers

Classically, this creates a free-rider problem where each player has an incentive to wait for someone else to act.

### 2. Quantum Extension

When players share entangled quantum resources:
- **Entanglement reduces the free-rider problem** by creating correlations between decisions
- Players can coordinate without classical communication
- The quantum equilibrium shifts toward higher volunteering probability
- **Shared entanglement acts as a coordination mechanism** that improves collective outcomes

### 3. Economic Implications

- **Mechanism design**: Entanglement can be used as a design variable in economic mechanisms
- **Collective action problems**: Quantum resources may help solve coordination failures
- **Market equilibria**: Quantum game theory extends classical equilibrium concepts
- **Auction design**: Quantum strategies may improve auction efficiency

## Application Patterns

### Quantum Coordination in Markets
```
Problem: Multiple agents need to coordinate but communication is limited
Solution: Share entangled quantum states to achieve correlated equilibria
Result: Higher collective utility than classical Nash equilibrium
```

### Free-Rider Mitigation
```
Problem: Public goods games suffer from free-riding behavior
Solution: Use quantum entanglement to correlate contribution decisions
Result: Increased contribution rates in the quantum equilibrium
```
