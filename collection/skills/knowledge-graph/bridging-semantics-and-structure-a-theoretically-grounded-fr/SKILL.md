# Bridging Semantics and Structure: A Theoretically Grounded Framework for Link Prediction on TAGs

**arXiv ID:** [2610.40192v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40192v1)
**Published:** 2026-10-29
**Authors:** Chen Shao, Ruirui Wang, Tobias Kafer, Danai Koutra
**Utility Score:** 0.95

## Abstract

Link prediction on text-attributed graphs (TAGs) is a fundamental problem with applications in knowledge discovery, recommendation and beyond. Recent progress has highlighted the promise of combining Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with Language Models (LMs) in order to leverage the complementary structural and semantic signals. However, due to the distinct inductive biases of GNNs and LMs, the design of an optimal fusion strategy remains an open research question. In this work, we present Bridge-LP, an intuitive, simple, efficient and theoretically grounded framework that unifies graph and language representations. It is built around three theoretically-justified design components: (D1) jumping connection of textual proximity, which enables robust LM training; (D2) compressed embedding, which encodes neighborhood information long exploited by classical link prediction heuristics, thereby enriching structural signals in a learnable way; and (D3) cache embedding training strategy, which reduces computational complexity. Together, these mechanisms establish an end-to-end cross-modal framework that scales to large graphs while capturing richer graph-text interactions. We conduct an extensive empirical evaluation, comparing against 40 existing models across 9 diverse datasets.
