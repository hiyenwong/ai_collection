# Lucid Dream Corpus: Large-Scale Dream Report Dataset

Based on: Mallett, R. (2026). "A large corpus of lucid and non-lucid dream reports." *arXiv:2603.26992*.

## Overview

- **55,000** dream reports from **5,000** contributors
- **10,000** lucid dream labels (user-categorized)
- **25,000** non-lucid dream labels
- **2,000** nightmare labels
- Data sourced from 10 years of public dream journal posts on an online forum
- Validated: language patterns in lucid-labeled reports match known characteristics of lucid dreams

## Key Findings

### Corpus Statistics
- Lucid dreams represent ~18% of labeled subset
- Lucid-labeled reports show distinct linguistic markers:
  - Higher first-person singular pronouns ("I", "me", "my")
  - More metacognitive language ("know", "realize", "aware")
  - Greater sensory-motor vocabulary
  - Increased temporal sequencing words

### Linguistic Validation
- Lucid dream reports contain more self-awareness markers
- Language analysis confirms construct validity of user labels
- Non-lucid reports show more passive voice and uncertainty markers
- Nightmare reports show distinct negative affect vocabulary
