# The Control Layer Behind Rule-Following Agents Trading Across Market Conditions

**Authors:** Saad Naja, Nasser Odtallah, Jake Van-Praag, Sarah May
**Published:** 2026-10-19
**arXiv:** [2610.26324v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26324v1)
**Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.MA, q-fin.RM, q-fin.TR
**Utility Score:** 1.0

## Abstract

Trading discipline often fails people. Our study tested whether four AI trading agents followed their strategies and risk rules. Offering traders the evidence and an agent evaluation toolkit to separate luck from following the rules, before giving agents meaningful autonomy or capital. Our study analysed 2,627 closed positions from 12 February to 27 August 2026 across the same asset universe and execution conditions. Most positions were simulated against live prices with a 0.09% round-trip fee; 18% involved real Hyperliquid orders. Each position declared a strategy, stop and target, with a limit of five open positions per agent. The fleet finished net positive after fees. Rule adherence was high across all four agents, including the unprofitable one. Profitability tracked how often agents won relative to their break-even win rates. Institutional-style controls ran by default and contained losses through a 29% market drawdown and recovery, with documented exceptions. The discipline held for all four agents. Agents kept 97.5% of gross loss within declared stops, and typical losses closed at declared levels. Declared risk remained stable after losses and broadly stable across calm and volatile months. Twenty-three of 24 runs respected the position limit; logs identify the exception's size and duration. The same live data produced different results across assigned strategies.
