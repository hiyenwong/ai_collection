# Algebraic World Model: From Exploration to Proofs

**Authors:** Guancheng Pan, Qixin Tan, Yang Yuan, Andrew Chi-Chih Yao
**Published:** 2026-10-29
**arXiv:** [2610.40503v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40503v1)
**Categories:** cs.AI, cs.LO
**Utility Score:** 0.94

## Abstract

AI has made remarkable progress in mathematics, solving challenging problems with machine-checkable proofs in Lean, yet typically relying on predefined mathematical definitions and formal specifications. This raises a natural question: can it reason rigorously about an unknown world? We introduce the Algebraic World Model (AWM), a framework that turns interaction with unfamiliar environments into explicit mathematical models supporting formal reasoning. AWM explores the environment and develops executable Python hypotheses, continually testing and revising them against observations. It then structures these hypotheses into typed objects and shared rules, simplifies their structure through category-theory-guided transformations, and formalizes the resulting model in Lean 4 to prove reachability and impossibility. AWM can formally prove that a goal is unreachable, even beyond explored trajectories. On two impossible game levels, it constructs closed, kernel-checked Lean proofs excluding every legal winning sequence in all six runs, while Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, equipped with coding tools, produce none in twelve baseline runs. Four of AWM's six initial bounds are correct, compared with one for Opus 5.5 and none for GPT-6 Astra; given only feedback that a cheaper win exists, AWM revises its rules and constructs new proofs, reaching six of six correct bounds.
