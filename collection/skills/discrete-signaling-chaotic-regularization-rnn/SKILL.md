## Abstract
Cortical circuits operate in a regime of intrinsic chaos, where even tiny changes in input can lead to divergent neural responses. Yet, remarkably, population codes in the brain vary smoothly with sensory stimuli, forming coherent representational manifolds. How can chaotic networks sustain such stable coding? Here, we develop a theoretical framework that links the microscopic chaos of recurrent networks to the macroscopic geometry of neural representations. Combining kernel methods with dynamical mean-field theory, we show that chaotic dynamics induce local roughness (introducing sharp distortions at small scales) while preserving global smoothness across larger stimulus variations. This structural property acts as an intrinsic regularizer, enhancing generalization while maintaining expressivity. Moreover, we show how chaotic networks naturally produce power-law spectral signatures, closely matching experimental observations in cortical recordings.
