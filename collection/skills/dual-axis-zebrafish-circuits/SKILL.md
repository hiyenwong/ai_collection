# Dual-Axis Attribution of Zebrafish Tectal Microcircuits

## Core Concept

Biological neural circuits contain specialized substructures supporting distinct computational functions. Many bio-inspired networks borrow biological motifs without identifying their circuit-to-function mappings.

### Two Computational Axes

1. **Energy Efficiency**: Optimized metabolic cost of neural computation
2. **Robustness**: Resilience to perturbations and noise

These axes are jointly optimized in the zebrafish tectal microcircuit, providing a blueprint for efficient neurocomputing.

### Key Insight

The tectal microcircuit achieves both energy efficiency and robustness through specialized substructures, suggesting that bio-inspired architectures should explicitly map circuit motifs to computational functions rather than blindly borrowing biological patterns.

## Application to Neural Architecture Design

### Circuit-to-Function Mapping

1. Identify specialized substructures in biological circuits
2. Map each substructure to specific computational function
3. Design artificial architectures that preserve the dual-axis optimization

### Energy-Robustness Trade-off

Biological circuits naturally balance energy efficiency with robustness. Artificial systems can learn from this dual-axis optimization rather than treating them as competing objectives.
