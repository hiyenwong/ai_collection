# State of Brain Emulation Report 2025

> Comprehensive 2025 reassessment of whole brain emulation (WBE) progress, evaluating advances in scanning technology, simulation capability, neurobiology understanding, and computational requirements since the seminal 2008 Sandberg and Bostrom roadmap.

## Metadata
- **Source**: arXiv:2510.15745
- **Published**: 2025-10 (v3 updated)
- **Category**: cs.AI

## Core Methodology

### Key Innovation
Systematic evaluation of the entire WBE pipeline from tissue preparation through scanning, reconstruction, simulation, and validation. Provides updated timelines and identifies key bottlenecks that have shifted since 2008, offering a realistic assessment of when whole brain emulation might become feasible.

### Technical Framework
1. **Scanning Technology**: Progress in electron microscopy, expansion microscopy, and X-ray tomography
2. **Reconstruction Pipeline**: Automated segmentation, synapse detection, and circuit mapping
3. **Simulation Requirements**: Computational budgets for different levels of abstraction
4. **Neurobiology Gaps**: Understanding of synaptic plasticity, neuromodulation, glial contributions
5. **Validation Strategies**: How to verify that an emulation faithfully reproduces brain function

## Implementation Guide

### Prerequisites
- Background in computational neuroscience
- Understanding of connectomics and neuroanatomy
- High-performance computing resources

### Step-by-Step
1. Assess current scanning resolution vs. required resolution for WBE
2. Evaluate automated reconstruction accuracy and throughput
3. Model computational requirements at different simulation abstraction levels
4. Identify neurobiological unknowns that affect emulation fidelity
5. Develop validation benchmarks for emulation quality

## Applications
- Roadmap planning for large-scale neuroscience initiatives
- Resource allocation for brain simulation projects
- Identifying high-impact research directions in connectomics
- Informing AI architecture design through brain emulation insights
- Digital twin brain development

## Pitfalls
- WBE feasibility depends on unresolved questions in neuroscience
- Computational requirements may be underestimated at cellular resolution
- Ethical considerations of brain emulation are substantial
- Progress in scanning does not guarantee progress in simulation
