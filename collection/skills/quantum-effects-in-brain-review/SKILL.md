## Key Framework: Evaluating Quantum Brain Claims

```python
def evaluate_quantum_brain_claim(
    mechanism,           # Proposed quantum mechanism
    decoherence_time,     # Estimated decoherence time
    biological_timescale, # Relevant biological process timescale
    experimental_evidence # Current empirical support
):
    """Evaluate plausibility of quantum brain claim."""
    
    # Primary criterion: decoherence time vs biological timescale
    # Warm, wet brain environment causes rapid decoherence
    # Quantum effect must persist long enough to be functional
    
    ratio = decoherence_time / biological_timescale
    
    assessment = {
        'timescale_viable': ratio > 0.01,  # Must be >1% of biological timescale
        'experimental_support': experimental_evidence,
        'overall_plausibility': 'high' if (ratio > 0.1 and experimental_evidence == 'strong')
                                else 'medium' if ratio > 0.01
                                else 'low'
    }
    return assessment
```

## The Decoherence Challenge

The central objection to quantum brain theories:
- Brain is warm (~310K) and wet
- Decoherence times estimated at ~10⁻¹³ to 10⁻²⁰ seconds
- Neural processes occur at ~10⁻³ seconds (millisecond scale)
- Gap of 10-17 orders of magnitude

### Counterarguments from Quantum Biology
- Photosynthesis maintains coherence at room temperature
- Biological systems may have evolved quantum-protective mechanisms
- Local ordering (e.g., in microtubule interiors) could extend coherence

## Three Major Models (Unified View)

arXiv: 2301.09569 proposes a unified quantum-classical model incorporating:
1. **EM field theory**: Electromagnetic field dynamics
2. **Orch-OR**: Objective reduction in microtubules
3. **Dissipative quantum model**: Open quantum system dynamics

Uses Mixed Weyl symbol to bridge microscopic quantum processes with mesoscopic measurements.
