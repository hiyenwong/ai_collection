# NeuroAI and Beyond: Bridging Neuroscience and AI

> NSF workshop report identifying three fundamental capability gaps in current AI and proposing neuroscience-inspired solutions for embodied intelligence, continual learning, and energy-efficient computation.

## Metadata
- **Source**: arXiv:2604.18637
- **Authors**: Anthony Zador, Jean-Marc Fellous, Terrence Sejnowski, et al.
- **Published**: 2026-04-19
- **Workshop**: National Science Foundation, August 2025

## Core Framework

### Three Fundamental AI Capability Gaps

The NSF workshop identified critical gaps in current AI systems:

| Gap | Problem | Neuroscience Solution |
|-----|---------|----------------------|
| **Physical World Interaction** | AI cannot effectively interact with physical environments | Co-design of body and brain for embodied intelligence |
| **Brittle Learning** | AI systems are fragile and fail with distribution shift | Continual learning via replay and synaptic consolidation |
| **Energy/Data Inefficiency** | Unsustainable resource consumption | Spike-based computation and event-driven processing |

### Neuroscience Principles for AI

#### 1. Embodied Intelligence (Body-Brain Co-design)
- **Principle**: Intelligence emerges from bidirectional body-brain interaction
- **Key Insight**: Physical morphology shapes computational requirements
- **Implementation**: Co-evolve sensorimotor systems with neural controllers
- **Applications**: Robotics, autonomous systems, human-computer interaction

#### 2. Continual Learning
- **Principle**: Biological systems learn continuously without catastrophic forgetting
- **Mechanisms**:
  - **Replay**: Hippocampal replay consolidates memories during rest/sleep
  - **Synaptic Consolidation**: Protect important synapses during new learning
  - **Structural Plasticity**: Physical rewiring enables new capabilities
- **Implementation**: Combine replay buffers with importance-weighted parameter updates

#### 3. Spike-Based Computation
- **Principle**: Neural systems use sparse, event-driven communication
- **Advantages**:
  - Energy proportional to information (not clock cycles)
  - Natural temporal coding
  - Robust to component failure
- **Implementation**: Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) on neuromorphic hardware

## Research Agenda

### Priority Areas

1. **Embodied AI Development**
   - Design robots with morphology suited to their environment
   - Integrate proprioceptive feedback into learning
   - Develop bio-inspired sensors (event cameras, tactile skin)

2. **Lifelong Learning Systems**
   - Implement sleep-inspired consolidation mechanisms
   - Design modular architectures that allow skill composition
   - Create evaluation benchmarks for continual learning

3. **Neuromorphic Computing**
   - Scale spiking networks to match DNN capabilities
   - Develop training algorithms for event-based systems
   - Build hardware that exploits temporal sparsity

### Cross-Cutting Themes

- **Interdisciplinary Collaboration**: Neuroscientists, computer scientists, engineers
- **Hardware-Software Co-design**: Algorithms matched to physical substrates
- **Benchmark Development**: Tasks that capture real-world challenges
- **Ethical Considerations**: Safety, alignment, and societal impact

## Implementation Guide

### For AI Researchers
1. Study biological systems relevant to your problem domain
2. Identify computational principles, not just surface similarities
3. Test on embodied tasks with physical constraints
4. Measure energy efficiency alongside accuracy

### For Neuroscientists
1. Identify mechanisms that solve computational problems
2. Characterize at algorithmic level, not just implementation
3. Collaborate on testable predictions in artificial systems
4. Use AI tools to analyze complex neural data

### For Engineers
1. Design hardware that supports event-driven computation
2. Build sensors that match biological efficiency
3. Create development platforms for embodied AI
4. Optimize for deployment constraints (power, latency, size)

## Key Insights

### Why Neuroscience Matters for AI
- **Evolution as Algorithm Designer**: 3.5 billion years of optimization
- **Existence Proofs**: Brain demonstrates what's possible
- **Constraint Satisfaction**: Biology reveals efficient solutions
- **Novel Approaches**: Paradigms beyond current engineering practice

### Beyond Deep Learning
- Need for active learning (curiosity-driven exploration)
- Importance of world models and predictive coding
- Role of neuromodulation in regulating plasticity
- Value of sparse, distributed representations
