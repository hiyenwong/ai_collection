# What 81,000 People Want from AI

## Overview

The largest and most multilingual qualitative study ever conducted on AI user expectations. Over one week, 80,508 people across 159 countries and 70 languages participated in conversational interviews conducted by an AI-powered interviewer. This methodology bridges the tradeoff between depth and volume in qualitative research. Complementary economic analysis examined how users' professional roles, income, and geography shaped their AI usage, expectations, and concerns.

## Methodology

### Conversational AI Interviewer
- Version of Claude prompted to conduct open-ended, adaptive interviews
- Asked predetermined questions about hopes and concerns, adapted follow-ups based on responses
- Bridges the qualitative depth vs. volume tradeoff
- Conducted in 70 languages across 159 countries

### Analysis Pipeline
- Claude-powered classifiers categorized conversations across dimensions: hopes, fears, profession, satisfaction, sentiment
- "What people want" classified into single primary category per respondent
- Concerns multi-label coded (interviews could receive multiple concern codes)
- Representative quotes extracted by Claude, manually reviewed for PII removal

### Privacy & Ethics
- Users informed responses would be used for research
- Option to publish de-identified responses
- All responses de-identified before analysis
- Quotes further manually reviewed for any identifying details
- Answers reflective of AI usage broadly (not just Claude); competitor names redacted

## Key Findings: Hopes & Aspirations

| Category | % | Description |
|----------|---|-------------|
| Professional Excellence | 19% | AI handles mundane tasks so they can focus on strategic work |
| Knowledge & Growth | 14% | AI as learning companion, expanding understanding |
| Personal Efficiency | 12% | Streamlining personal life, reducing friction |
| Creativity | 11% | AI as creative partner and amplifier |
| Connection | 9% | AI helping connect with others, overcome loneliness |
| Entrepreneurship | 9% | AI as business-building partner |
| Wellbeing | 8% | AI for health, mental wellness support |
| Meaning | 8% | Deeper purpose, using AI for what matters most |
| Fun & Play | 7% | Entertainment, play, curiosity |

### Deeper Pattern: "Seeing the Forest"
Many users started talking about productivity, but when probed about underlying motivations, deeper values surfaced — more time with family, reading more books, improved quality of life. AI efficiency was a means, not an end.

## Key Findings: Concerns

- **Loss of human skills** — Am I losing my ability to think for myself?
- **Job displacement** — Will AI replace me?
- **Loss of connection** — AI making us more isolated
- **Misinformation & control** — AI used to manipulate
- **Dependence** — Becoming too reliant on AI

## Key Economics Findings

- **AI adoption is broad**: 25% of current AI use displaces human labor (automation), 43% augments it (complementarity), 32% neither
- **Lower-wage workers more likely automated**: ~50% of lower-wage tasks automated vs. ~25% of higher-wage
- **Programmers most displaced**: 27% automation rate per task for programming occupations
- **International variations**: High-income countries use AI more for coding/writing; lower-income for translation, education, and general assistance
- **AI complements creative/analytical work**: Translators and writers see high augmentation rates
- **Concentration of economic impacts**: 80% of automation and augmentation concentrated in tech, creative, and professional services

## Reusable Patterns

### Pattern: AI-Powered Large-Scale Qualitative Research
1. Use adaptive AI interviewer for open-ended conversation
2. Apply AI classifiers for systematic coding/thematic analysis
3. Preserve depth through adaptive follow-up questions
4. Scale to 80k+ participants across 70+ languages

### Pattern: AI Adoption Economics Analysis
1. Classify AI use cases into automation vs. augmentation vs. neutral
2. Cross-reference with occupation, income, geography
3. Identify displacement and complementarity patterns
4. Track usage shift over time for economic impact monitoring
