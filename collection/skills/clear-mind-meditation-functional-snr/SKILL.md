## Core Contribution

Proposes **functional signal-to-noise ratio (f-SNR)** as a unifying construct for meditation research. Signal = neural variance tracking goal-relevant causes of sensory input; Noise = residual activity including irrelevant endogenous fluctuations. Maps diverse meditation findings onto two primary operations: signal enhancement and noise decluttering.

## Theoretical Framework

### f-SNR Definition
- **Signal**: Neural variance that tracks goal-relevant causes of sensory input
- **Noise**: Residual activity, including irrelevant endogenous fluctuations
- **f-SNR**: Ratio of task-relevant neural signal to background noise
- **Key distinction**: Not just overall SNR, but *functional* SNR relative to task/goal

### Two Primary Operations of Meditation

#### 1. Signal Enhancement
- Selectively boosting neural responses to goal-relevant stimuli
- Mechanisms: attentional focus, top-down增益 modulation
- Measurable via: increased evoked responses, improved decoding accuracy

#### 2. Noise Decluttering
- Reducing irrelevant neural activity
- Mechanisms: suppressing default mode network, reducing mind-wandering
- Measurable via: neural variability quenching, reduced background power

### Depth Effect: Critical Regime Shift
Deepening meditation practice proposed to:
1. **Reduce self-referential filtering** — less DMN dominance
2. **Shift toward critical regime** — thermodynamically efficient state
3. **Maximize information transmission** — optimal dynamic range
4. **Balance stability and flexibility** — edge of chaos dynamics

## Empirical Evidence Base

### Supporting Metrics
The framework connects to existing measurable phenomena:
- **Neural variability quenching**: Reduced trial-to-trial variability during focused attention
- **Mutual information**: Increased information transfer between relevant brain regions
- **Multivariate decoding**: Improved classification of stimuli/states in experienced meditators

### Transdiagnostic Explanation
Meditation efficacy across psychopathologies explained by low-SNR states:
- Depression: rumination as noise (self-referential filtering)
- Anxiety: threat-signal amplification with reduced signal specificity
- ADHD: poor signal enhancement + excessive noise
- PTSD: traumatic memories as dominant noise source
- **Prediction**: Meditation increases f-SNR across all these conditions

## Key Predictions

### Testable Hypotheses
1. **Neural variability quenching** should correlate with meditation depth
2. **Mutual information** between sensory and task-relevant areas should increase
3. **Multivariate decoding accuracy** should improve with training
4. **Critical dynamics signatures** (power-law scaling, maximal dynamic range) should emerge in experts
5. **BCI performance** should improve in experienced meditators

### BCI Implications
Meditation may improve brain-computer interfaces by:
- Making neural signals more readable (higher f-SNR)
- Reducing noise from mind-wandering
- Enhancing voluntary modulation of neural activity
- **Practical implication**: Pre-session meditation training for BCI users

## Technical Details

### Critical Regime Characterization
- **Thermodynamic efficiency**: Maximum information per unit energy
- **Dynamic range**: Maximum range of distinguishable input intensities
- **Signatures**: Power-law avalanche distributions, 1/f spectral slope
- **Balance**: Neither too ordered (epileptic) nor too chaotic (noisy)

### Neural Variability Quenching (NVQ)
- Stimulus presentation reduces ongoing variability
- Stronger NVQ = better f-SNR
- Meditation training should enhance NVQ
- Links to predictive coding: reduced prediction error variance

### Self-Referential Filtering
- Default Mode Network (DMN) as source of self-referential noise
- Meditation reduces DMN dominance
- Less self-referential filtering = more "direct" sensory processing
- Connection to "pure awareness" experiences in contemplative traditions

## Methodology for Testing

### Recommended Measurements
1. **f-SNR operationalization**: 
   - Signal power: task-relevant neural encoding (e.g., stimulus decoding accuracy)
   - Noise power: residual variance after removing task-relevant component
   - f-SNR = signal_power / noise_power

2. **Meditation depth markers**:
   - Self-reported depth (experience sampling)
   - Physiological markers (respiratory rate, HRV)
   - Neural markers (DMN suppression, NVQ magnitude)

3. **Critical regime detection**:
   - Neuronal avalanche analysis (power-law exponent)
   - Spectral analysis (1/f slope)
   - Dynamic range measurement (input-output function)

### Experimental Designs
- **Cross-sectional**: Compare novice/intermediate/expert meditators
- **Longitudinal**: Track f-SNR changes during MBSR/retreat training
- **Intervention**: Pre-session meditation training for BCI users
- **Clinical**: f-SNR as mediator of meditation effects on symptoms

## Limitations and Caveats

### Current Limitations
- Framework is largely theoretical; needs systematic empirical testing
- f-SNR operationalization needs standardization
- Meditation heterogeneity makes testing challenging
- Critical regime claims need rigorous physics validation

### Falsification Criteria
Framework would be undermined if:
1. Expert meditators show NO improvement in f-SNR metrics
2. Critical regime signatures are absent in long-term practitioners
3. BCI performance does NOT improve with meditation training
4. Clinical improvements are NOT mediated by f-SNR changes
