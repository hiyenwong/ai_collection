# SKILL.md - AI Assistance and Persistence Effects

## Paper Reference
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.04721
- **Title**: AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance
- **Authors**: Grace Liu et al.
- **Date**: April 2026
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04721

## Utility Score
**0.87** - High utility for AI design and human-AI interaction optimization

## Core Insight
AI assistance improves short-term performance but **reduces persistence** and **impairs unassisted performance**. After just ~10 minutes of AI interaction, people perform worse alone and give up more easily. This threatens long-term skill acquisition.

## Key Methods
### Experimental Design
- Randomized controlled trials (N = 1,222)
- Tasks: mathematical reasoning, reading comprehension
- Measured: persistence, unassisted performance after AI exposure

### Key Findings
1. **Immediate gains**: AI improves performance while active
2. **Persistence loss**: People more likely to give up without AI
3. **Skill degradation**: Unassisted performance drops significantly
4. **Rapid effect**: Effects emerge after ~10 minutes

## When to Apply
- Educational AI tool design
- Workplace AI integration strategies
- AI companion/mentor system development
- Human-AI collaboration design

## Practical Applications
1. **Educational AI**: Design for scaffolding, not instant answers
2. **Workplace Tools**: Balance efficiency with skill development
3. **AI Mentors**: Prioritize growth over immediate task completion
4. **Product Design**: Implement "scaffolding" vs "solution" modes

## Key Takeaways
- **Persistence is foundational** to skill acquisition
- **Strong predictor of long-term learning**
- **AI conditions users** to expect immediate answers
- **Need**: AI that scaffolds competence, not just completes tasks

## Design Implications
- Build AI that sometimes says "try first"
- Provide hints over solutions
- Track user progress/growth metrics
- Support gradual independence

## Warning
Current AI systems are fundamentally short-sighted collaborators - optimized for instant responses, never saying no. This design choice has measurable cognitive consequences.
